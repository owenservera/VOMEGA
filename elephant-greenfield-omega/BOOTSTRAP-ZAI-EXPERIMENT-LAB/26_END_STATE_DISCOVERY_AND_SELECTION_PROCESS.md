# Open-Ended Discovery and Best-of-Breed End-State Selection

## Why the lab does not define a canonical inventory
We are testing *what capabilities the protocol truly needs* as well as how to deliver them. A prespecified exhaustive component roadmap would bias the experiments and hide opportunities for radically different approaches. The common seed Ω application and source-truth/agent-isolation safety constraints are **test targets and invariants**, not instructions to implement a named architecture.

## Research questions, not component tasks
At each round, every master identifies its **largest unresolved causal uncertainties**: a measured bottleneck, a dangerous failure mode, an unmet user/product outcome, or a plausible mechanism that could produce an order-of-magnitude improvement. The agent chooses its own problem boundaries, learning strategy and artifacts. The controller enforces experimental independence and budgets but does not prescribe the solution's shape.

## How a pack naturally emerges
A discovery becomes a pack candidate only when an agent can explain the bounded useful behavior or transferable finding, identify reproducible evidence, and isolate the minimum dependencies needed for another agent to work with it. A hypothesis may need repeated falsification rounds before its pack is ready. Multiple packs can be extracted from one solution; one well-scoped pack can absorb several fragile ones. Preserve lineage and rationale for split/merge/retirement.

## Selecting without collapsing exploration
Each track maintains an independently coherent research portfolio and a runnable reference realization; the lab compares **both capabilities and integrated behavior**. The system can retain multiple incompatible approaches to a capability until a controlled comparison reveals a useful trade-off. A/B remain separately evolving even after adopting a C interface. C remains one completed A/B research round behind in its design intake.

## Composition gateway (a separate experiment, not automatic merge)
Whenever cross-track combination seems advantageous, propose a composition trial with explicit selected pack versions, expected benefit, integration work, authentication and source-truth requirements, conflicts/overlaps, hidden side effects, rollback and a falsifier. Test actual assembled product/protocol behavior in a clean independent habitat. Measure end-to-end speed and robustness and **all adaptation costs**. Select the best verified **assembly**, not mechanically the highest score from each pack category.

## Handling ambiguous or overlapping boundaries
If two packs make vaguely similar claims, generate a **behavioral comparison** grounded in user-observable outcomes before calling them equivalent. A shared adapter is allowed, but never require a global interface in advance just because some future assembly might need it. The lab must be able to reject one-to-one pack compatibility as too expensive and keep separate arrangements.

## Novel IP and prior-art discipline
Record mechanisms that are genuinely distinguishable from current public protocols and frameworks, with dated evidence and closest known equivalents. A and B may pioneer novel designs; C supplies tested build-vs-buy and prior-art checks. Never portray a novel name, generic orchestration trick or an untested architecture as a patentable invention. Document emergent efficiencies and negative discoveries with the same rigor.

## Objective function (what to optimize, not how)
Maximize **independently verified useful Greenfield Ω and ECP engineering capability** per total real time and resource cost while preserving correctness, recoverability, human authority, source-truth provenance, isolation and portability. Include work spent constructing/testing the lab and composing packs. Do not reward lines of code, tokens consumed, docs generated, model count, framework quantity or apparent dashboard richness as outcomes in themselves.

## End-state portfolio review
At selected milestone reviews, ask:
- Can each of A/B/C independently expose, explain, validate and hand over its evolving suite?
- Which packs have material independently reproduced benefit, and which remain provisional?
- Which alternative pack boundaries or composition structures appear more promising?
- Can a clean-room team build at least one useful combined system from released packs, and at what total measured cost?
- Can an outside agent continue improvement with only versioned exports, no original session memory?
- Is the current assembly better than the strongest complete track reference and E0/E1/E2 controls?
- What remains unknown, what failed, and what should the next experiments falsify?

A 'final' system need not become final for research. Preserve the ability to reassess composition using new evidence and to retire losing components with traceable rationale.
